Four phones. A Mars base in a dust storm. Survive 90 seconds.
The only player who can touch the controls can't hear you, and a hacker is sending her fake orders.

How a round goes

  1. Things break. Each crew member sees a different piece of the problem, so you have to talk out loud to work out what's wrong.
  2. When it's your problem, press your button. An order card fills Vega's screen. Vega can't hear you, so the card is the only way to tell her.
  3. Every card carries a stamp. Vega checks it before she does anything: SEALED means do it, anything else means ignore it.
  4. With about 30 seconds left on the clock, the hacker steals one crew member's key. Its fake cards now show SEALED too. The only clue is a NOT YOU line in the victim's own log, so they have to yell it.
  5. Chen sends ROTATE for that seat. Vega grabs the key token from the Spine and runs it to Comms. Now the stolen key is dead.
  6. At 90 seconds you get an incident report: what got through, what you caught, and how fast you killed the stolen key.

The stamp on every card

SEALEDSigned by the console it names. Real. Do it.
BROKEN SEALThe signature doesn't check out. The hacker wrote it. Ignore it.
OLD COUNTERA real card, recorded and played back again. Ignore it.

Try it: you're Vega. Would you obey?

Why it's more than a party game

REAL CRYPTOEvery card is signed on the phone with HMAC-SHA256 and verified on the server. The stamp is the check.
REAL ATTACKSForgery, replay, a stolen credential, and revocation: the four things security teams drill.
REAL ASYMMETRYEach phone is only ever sent its own slice of the game. Vega's phone never receives what she isn't allowed to know.
